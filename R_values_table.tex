\section*{Definitions}
The \emph{keys} of a positive integer $n$ are the fractions $a/b$ in lowest terms with $1\le a\le n$ and
$2\le b\le n$. These are the fractions that may be used as parts. The number of keys of $n$ is denoted by $K(n)$.
A \emph{rational partition} of $n$ is a multiset of keys of $n$ whose sum is $n$, and $R(n)$ is the number of
rational partitions of $n$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rlrr}
\toprule
$n$ & keys of $n$ & $K(n)$ & $R(n)$\\
\midrule
1 & none & 0 & 0\\
2 & $1/2$ & 1 & 1\\
3 & $1/3,\ 1/2,\ 2/3,\ 3/2$ & 4 & 16\\
4 & $1/4,\ 1/3,\ 1/2,\ 2/3,\ 3/4,\ 4/3,\ 3/2$ & 7 & 226\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For example, the only rational partition of $2$ is $2=\frac12+\frac12+\frac12+\frac12$, and
$3=\frac32+\frac12+\frac23+\frac13$ is one of the $16$ rational partitions of $3$.
The number $1$ has no keys, so $K(1)=0$ and $R(1)=0$. The number of keys is
\[
 K(n)=2\bigl(\varphi(1)+\varphi(2)+\dots+\varphi(n)\bigr)-n-1,
\]
where $\varphi$ is Euler's function, so $K(n)$ is about $6n^2/\pi^2$. For $n\ge2$ every key of $n$ occurs in at
least one rational partition of $n$: take $b$ copies of $a/b$, which add up to $a$, and fill the rest with
copies of $\frac12$.

More generally, let $R_N(m)$ be the number of multisets of keys of $N$ whose sum is $m$, so that $R(n)=R_n(n)$.
Then $R_N(m)$ is the coefficient of $z^m$ in the product of $1/(1-z^{a/b})$ over the keys $a/b$ of $N$.
In the paper \emph{Asymptotics and limit laws for diagonal rational partitions}, called the paper below, the set
of keys of $N$ is denoted by $\mathcal C_N$, and $R(n)$ is considered for $n\ge2$.

\section*{Asymptotic formulas}
Which asymptotic formula is the best depends on what is asked. The best proved formula in closed form is the
theorem below. The most accurate formula is the sharp formula after it, and only part of that one is proved.

\subsection*{The theorem}
As $n\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{thm}
 \log R(n)=\sqrt2\,n^{3/2}-\kappa\,\frac{n^{3/2}}{\log n}-\kappa_2\,\frac{n^{3/2}}{\log^2n}
 +O\Bigl(\frac{n^{3/2}}{\log^3n}\Bigr),\qquad \kappa=0.0026471306\ldots,\quad \kappa_2=0.0010771199\ldots
\end{equation}
This is Theorem~1.1 of the paper, where the two constants are given by explicit integrals. It is the best proved
formula in closed form. The same theorem contains a second formula with the smaller error
$O(n^{5/4}/\log n)$. It is the minimum over one real variable of an expression that contains a sum over the
primes up to $n$, and it is not reproduced here.

The theorem describes the limit and is not meant for the $n$ of this table. By the formula below, $\log R(n)$
also contains terms of order $n$, the largest being $-\frac32\log\lcm(1,\dots,n)$. They are of smaller order
than the error term, so the theorem does not show them. For example, for $n=100$ the three terms give
$1413.59$ and the exact value is $1272.94$. The difference is $140.65$, and
$\frac32\log\lcm(1,\dots,100)=141.07$.

\subsection*{The sharp formula}
Let $\beta>0$. Give every key $x$ of $n$ an independent multiplicity that is geometric with ratio $e^{-\beta x}$,
and put $F_n(\beta)=-\sum_x\log(1-e^{-\beta x})$, where $x$ runs over the keys of $n$.
For a prime $\ell\le n$ let $q$ be the largest power of $\ell$ not exceeding $n$. The \emph{block} of $\ell$ is
the set of keys of $n$ whose denominator is divisible by $q$. Let $P_\ell(\beta)$ be the probability that the sum
of the parts taken from the block has a denominator that is not divisible by $q$. This must happen if the total
is to be an integer. With $k=\lfloor n/q\rfloor$ and $w(x)=q\,\lcm(1,\dots,k)\,x$, which is an integer for every
$x$ in the block,
\[
 P_\ell(\beta)=\frac1\ell\sum_{r=0}^{\ell-1}\ \prod_{x\text{ in the block of }\ell}
 \frac{1-e^{-\beta x}}{1-e^{2\pi irw(x)/\ell-\beta x}} .
\]
Put
\[
 \Psi_n(\beta)=\beta n+F_n(\beta)+\sum_{\ell\le n}\log P_\ell(\beta),
\]
where $\ell$ runs over the primes. Let $\beta^*$ be the point where $\Psi_n$ is smallest, let
$\sigma^2=\Psi_n''(\beta^*)$, and let $D_n$ be $\lcm(1,\dots,n)$ divided by the product of the primes up to $n$.
The formula is
\begin{equation}\label{sharp}
 R(n)\approx\frac{e^{\Psi_n(\beta^*)}}{D_n\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}\,(1+c_2),\qquad
 c_2=\frac{\Psi_n''''(\beta^*)}{8\sigma^4}-\frac{5\,\Psi_n'''(\beta^*)^2}{24\,\sigma^6}.
\end{equation}
For example, for $n=2$ the only key is $\frac12$, and $\Psi_2(\beta)=2\beta-\log(1-e^{-\beta})$. Its minimum is
at $\beta^*=\log\frac32$, where $e^{\Psi_2}=\frac{27}4$, $\sigma^2=6$, $D_2=1$ and $c_2=-\frac7{72}$. The formula
gives $R(2)\approx0.992$, and $R(2)=1$.

What is proved and what is not:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \emph{Proved.} For every $\beta>0$, the number $R(n)$ equals $e^{\Psi_n(\beta)}$ times the probability
that the total is $n$, given that the condition above holds for every prime. So $\log R(n)\le\Psi_n(\beta)$ for
every $\beta$. With Proposition~4.1 of the paper this gives
$\Psi_n(\beta^*)-Cn\le\log R(n)\le\Psi_n(\beta^*)$ with a constant $C$. So $\log R(n)$ is known with an
error $O(n)$, and this is the most precise statement that is proved.
\item \emph{Conjecture.} Given the conditions, the total is a multiple of $1/D_n$, and for $\beta=\beta^*$ its
mean is $n$ and its variance is $\sigma^2$. The statement that the probability above is asymptotic to
$1/(D_n\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2})$ is a local limit theorem on this lattice, and it is not proved. Conjecture~8.1 of
the paper is the same statement with only the primes above $\sqrt n$ in the sum and the matching lattice.
For a prime $\ell$ below $\sqrt n$ the number $\ell P_\ell(\beta^*)$ is close to $1$, so the two forms should
agree in the limit, but for the $n$ of this table the difference is visible.
\item \emph{Numerical evidence only.} The factor $1+c_2$ is the next term of the local limit expansion. The
number $c_2$ is negative and of order $n^{-3/2}$: for $48\le n\le 101$ it lies between
$-0.31\,n^{-3/2}$ and $-0.26\,n^{-3/2}$, and for $n=100$ it is $-0.000279$. The smooth part
$\beta n+n^2/(2\beta)$ of $\Psi_n$ alone would give $-\frac{3}{8\sqrt2}\,n^{-3/2}$, which is
$-0.265\,n^{-3/2}$. This factor is not in the paper.
\end{itemize}

keys[n_] := Union[Select[Flatten[Table[a/b, {b, 2, n}, {a, 1, n}]], ! IntegerQ[#] &]];

(* Psi_n as a function of beta *)
psi[n_] := Module[{ks = keys[n], blocks},
   blocks = Table[Module[{q = l^(IntegerLength[n, l] - 1), xs, ws},
      xs = Select[ks, Divisible[Denominator[#], q] &];
      ws = Mod[q (LCM @@ Range[Quotient[n, q]]) xs, l];
      {l, xs, Table[N[Exp[2 Pi I Mod[r ws, l]/l], 40], {r, 0, l - 1}]}], {l, Prime[Range[PrimePi[n]]]}];
   Function[beta, beta n - Total[Log[1 - Exp[-beta ks]]] +
     Total[Function[blk, With[{t = Exp[-beta blk[[2]]]},
          Log[Re[Total[(Times @@ ((1 - t)/(1 - # t))) & /@ blk[[3]]]]/blk[[1]]]]] /@ blocks]]];

(* {minimiser, minimum of Psi_n, sigma^2, log of the formula without c2, log of the sharp formula} *)
logRSharp[n_] := Module[{f = psi[n], h = 10^-5, b, d1, d2, d3, d4, logD, v},
   b = N[Sqrt[n/2], 40];
   Do[d1 = (f[b + h] - f[b - h])/(2 h); d2 = (f[b + h] - 2 f[b] + f[b - h])/h^2;
    b = SetPrecision[Max[b - d1/d2, b/2], 40], {8}];
   d2 = (f[b + h] - 2 f[b] + f[b - h])/h^2;
   d3 = (f[b + 2 h] - 2 f[b + h] + 2 f[b - h] - f[b - 2 h])/(2 h^3);
   d4 = (f[b + 2 h] - 4 f[b + h] + 6 f[b] - 4 f[b - h] + f[b - 2 h])/h^4;
   logD = Log[(LCM @@ Range[n])/(Times @@ Prime[Range[PrimePi[n]]])];
   v = f[b] - logD - Log[2 Pi d2]/2;
   N[{b, f[b], d2, v, v + Log[1 + d4/(8 d2^2) - 5 d3^2/(24 d2^3)]}, 13]];

logRSharp[100]

logRTheorem[n_] := Sqrt[2] n^(3/2) - 0.0026471306 n^(3/2)/Log[n] - 0.0010771199 n^(3/2)/Log[n]^2;

\section*{Computation of the exact values}
The product is multiplied out with exact integers. The denominators are grouped by their largest prime factor,
and the primes are taken in decreasing order. Once all denominators with largest prime factor $p$ are multiplied
in, no later denominator is divisible by $p$, so every term whose exponent still has $p$ in its denominator is
dropped. For $n\le 101$ the exponents then lie on a lattice with denominator at most $27720$.
The program is \texttt{rational\_partitions.py}.

\section*{Checks}
All numbers in the table passed the following checks.
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item For all $n\le 101$: $K(n)$ recomputed as the number of distinct non-integral values of $a/b$ with
$1\le a,b\le n$, and from the formula above.
\item For $n\le8$: $R(n)$ counted from the definition with exact fractions, without generating functions.
\item For $n\le12$: comparison with a direct computation on the full lattice of exponents, for all $R_n(m)$ with $m\le n$.
\item For all $n\le 101$: the product recomputed modulo $2^{64}$ by a second implementation.
\item For all $n\le 101$: the value recomputed modulo $2^{64}$ from the formula
\[
 \prod_{a/b\in\mathcal C_N}\frac1{1-z^{a/b}}=\frac1{\mathcal P_N(z)}\prod_{d\le N}\ \prod_{j\le N/d}
 \mathcal P_{\lfloor N/d\rfloor}\bigl(z^{1/j}\bigr)^{\mu(d)},\qquad \mathcal P_K(z)=\prod_{k\le K}\frac1{1-z^k}.
\]
\item For $n\le10$ the values $R(n)$, and for $n\le60$ the values $K(n)$, recomputed in Mathematica from the definitions.
\item For $48\le n\le 101$: agreement with formula \eqref{sharp}, with a relative error below $3\cdot10^{-6}$. This check is of a
different kind, since the formula does not use the exact computation.
\end{enumerate}
Checks 1, 2, 4 and 5 are carried out by the program \texttt{verify.py}, and check 7 by \texttt{asymptotics.py}.
